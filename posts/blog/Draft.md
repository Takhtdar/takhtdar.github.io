# Task, Scheduling, and Bar

We all have tasks to do. reading this blog post for start is a task that you decided to do. Unlike many other tasks you have to do, paying attention to the following message isn't in your schedule. But if you want to plan for reading this later, there are at least two questions you need to answer. (a) on what date would you like to schedule this task for, (b) how would you know that date isn't fully occupied by a bunch of other tasks?

In past few weeks that I have been practicing scheduling my tasks in obsidian, I came across the second problem. *How I know a day in coming week has free space for a task to be scheduled*. There is a great list of plugins out there developed by greater community of Obsidian who deserve a shoutout. But I wanted something in my daily note, something that I can take a glance at and see how busy I am going to be at coming days.

![daily note](/resc/assets/img/20250916191747.jpg)
*bar showing allocated hours*

I don't like the idea of switching the window from my daily note to another window just to see if there is some free space in a near by date to be occupied by the coming task. problem was when I added a task I had two choice doing it today, or tomorrow. I didn't know what are my plans for three days from now, not without looking at this huge digital calendar screen. 
![calendar](/resc/assets/img/20250916192756.png)
*picture taken from: https://obsidian-community.github.io/obsidian-full-calendar/*

To solve my task scheduling problem in obsidian without having to look at a huge screen like any existing calendar, I came up with a simple yet effective way to see my scheduled list after tomorrow. I called it the bar, you can call it whatever you like as long as you actually use it. it's a bar (such a surprise) that have been divided to seven part by default, each part represent a day except for first one which represent today and any earlier task. depending on how many tasks you have allocated for a certain date the color of each part (day/date) change.

by no mean this script is completed, and scientifically and research based proven. it's just a simple widget that I use to pick a date for my next task in a glance. to get the code block feel free to email me, or send me a direct message in x/twitter.
